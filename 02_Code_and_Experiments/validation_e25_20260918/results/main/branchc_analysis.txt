DECISION
================================================================================================================

    Two halves, and the round had to report both because they disagree:

      (1) IS THERE ANYTHING TO RECLAIM?  The plan's own measure answers yes, on every
          corpus -- 25.71%-29.87% of container capacity sits above the live count.
      (2) IS THE RECLAIM DURABLE?  The re-growth counters answer no -- containers return
          to their own peak after 41.82%-49.48% of declines in W2 and 88.13%-97.83% in W1.

    kind        plan A+B      A/cap       B/cap   regrow/drop   disposition
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    mixed         29.48%     28.07% 0.63%-1.41%        46.07%   does not enter / B negligible
    skew          29.22%     27.68% 0.75%-1.54%        49.48%   does not enter / B negligible
    tranco        28.22%     22.95% 2.63%-5.26%        41.82%   does not enter / B present

    VERDICT: does NOT enter. The reason is recorded as the DURABILITY of the
    reclaim, NOT as an absence of one. The earlier draft's verdict -- 'nothing to
    reclaim, close as a negative result' -- is refuted by this run's own data: the
    plan's measure is 25.71%-29.87% on every corpus, and E24's 22%-30% is that same quantity.
    What E24's wording ('records decreased but the container still retains') does not
    survive is the inference: the same ~21% appears at a checkpoint where NOT ONE
    container has declined (mixed W1 'before': plan measure 21.28%, Sigma B = 0).

    Recorded separately rather than folded in, because the corpora disagree by 3-8x:
      tranco's Term B is the round's largest (see table) and over the tranco containers
      that actually declined it is larger still -- it is the one place where a
      shrink target below the historical peak would collect anything, and it is the
      only corpus whose B/cap trips the '5%' label threshold above.

    NOT MEASURED, and required before this candidate could be reopened on the
    shrink-to-peak policy: the rate at which a container sets a NEW high. Shrinking to
    the peak collects Term A (~29%) and only costs a reallocation when the container
    exceeds its all-time maximum -- an event this round did not instrument. The
    re-growth counter measures the return to an OLD peak, which under that policy is
    free. So this round rules out the reclaim as non-durable for the live-count
    target and leaves the peak target explicitly open rather than closed.
